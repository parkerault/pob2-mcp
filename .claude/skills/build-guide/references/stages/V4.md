# V4: one pass over what the build hinges on (`build-guide-verifier`)

Slice 03 §5.5 and §7.4, as amendment 4 changes them (D-352); spine C-30, D-142, D-351. **One verifier checks
the run in one pass, with no lenses:** every hinge claim of `plan.json` against its source, and `guide.md`
read like an editor against the plan and the final compile record. It writes its own report,
`reports/V4-<n>.md`, and never overwrites one. The ledger's arithmetic is the run checker's, which
re-evaluates every formula against the record: you check only that the entry a hinge claim cites models
what the claim says. The plan holds the facts; the guide renders them with no markers. No verifier wrote
anything it checks, and none can change it (D-142).

The **full pass** (SKILL.md step 11) runs once, after W1 writes the guide. After the fix round, and after a
checkpoint-2 revision, V4 runs once more as a **re-check** (step 13) of only what `check-run.mjs --changed
<n>` lists: the claims whose text, source or cited record values changed since the runner's copy in
`rounds/<n>/`, and the guide's changed H2s. **A revision runs no full pass**: its re-check covers what the
revision changed. After a fix round's re-check there is no further round: what stays open becomes a known
issue in the guide. After a revision's re-check, a fix round and its re-check may follow, as after the full
pass (SKILL.md step 16). The dossier isn't checked: it serves the verification gate's pick, and the guide
can't cite it (S3).

## Inputs

- **Header:** `stage: "V4"`, `check` (`full` or `recheck`), `attempt`, `concept` (the card the guide is for),
  `mode`, `runFolder`, `runFolderRel`, `compile: "none"`, and:
  - `writes`: `["reports/V4-<n>.md"]`, your report and the one file you write. `<n>` is this pass's number,
    one more than the last report's (1 for the run's first);
  - `findings`: `[]` for the full pass; for a re-check, the ids of the findings still open, the `unresolved`
    findings of the last report, `reports/V4-<n-1>.md`, where you read them;
  - `changed`: a re-check only: the lines `check-run.mjs --changed <n-1>` printed, as printed, one item
    each, `claim stages[<i>].claims[<k>] (<id>)` or `section <H2 title>`. Locations, never text.
- **Read** (paths relative to `runFolder`), only what you check:
  - **the claims of `plan.json`.** The plan can pass 90K tokens: never Read it whole. Grep it for `"claims"`
    with line numbers, and Read each stage's `claims` array from there to its closing `]`. Every stage has
    a `claims` array, so the hits come in stage order: the first is `stages[0]`'s, and a hit's place in that
    order is the `<i>` of a `location`, a claim's place in its array the `<k>`. A claim is `{id, text, kind,
    source}`; `kind` is `hinge` or `reason`. Grep a stage's `id` and `variant`, a `ledger[]` entry or a
    `prices[]` entry only where a claim needs it;
  - **`compile-record.json`**, the final compile's record: Grep only its measured values that a claim or a
    guide figure cites (`stages.<id>.outputs.<Field>`, `skillOutputs`, `measures`, the key figures), never
    Read it whole. Never its `ledger` values: after a ledger-only fix, which recompiles nothing, they are
    stale, and the run checker's `[ledger]` owns them, over the plan's current formulas (D-365);
  - **`guide.md`**: every H2 for the full pass, the `changed` H2s for a re-check;
  - `run-log.json` (`mode`), and the D1 gate's last verdict file under `reports/gates/` (its budget totals);
  - the committed C1/E1 files under `data/` that a claim cites (`data/campaign/`, `data/enemies/`), each
    fact with its `value`, `source` (an id in the file's `sources[]`), `patch` and `confidence`.

## Work

### What the pass checks

- **Every hinge claim, against its source.** A claim with `kind: "hinge"` states a fact whose being wrong
  would make the build fail at its rung (D-351); a claim with no `kind` is checked as one. Check it by its
  source's kind (below) and against the three claim rules.
- **A `reason` claim** rests on a measurement the compile made, and isn't checked against its source; the
  editor read holds the guide to it.
- **`guide.md`, read like an editor** (below), every H2.

### The claim rules

Every hinge claim keeps all three (`source-markers.md`, S7); a claim that breaks one is a finding:
1. **Supported in every part:** each number, mechanic and qualifier ("always", "on hit", "against bosses").
2. **It cites each fact it rests on:** a claim resting on two facts carries a source, or a ledger entry, for
   each.
3. **It carries its source's caveats:** a source of confidence M or L, a disputed fact, a `judgment`, or a
   mechanic no lookup confirmed is stated with that caveat, once in the guide, never later as settled.

### By source kind

1. **`external`**: online, open the source with WebFetch, only on D-171's hosts (poe2db.tw, maxroll.gg,
   game8.co, poe-vault.com, patch-note mirrors; never pathofexile.com or poe.ninja; a URL on another host is
   a finding, not a fetch), and check that it says what the claim says, for the patch the tag names.
   Offline, open nothing: an `external` claim in an offline run is an `unresolved` finding (it should be
   `external-unavailable`).
2. **`data`** (`data:<file>#<key>`): find the fact in `data/<file>` (and the `[<member>]` its key names) and
   check the claim against its value. Then check the fact's stored citation: a `pob-data` one with a lookup,
   or by reading that line of the pinned PoB checkout; an `external` one has its URL, `accessed` date and
   confidence, and **offline you don't fetch it** (D-232).
3. **`pob-data`**: re-check it with the lookup tools (repeat the lookup its ref names, or look up the fact
   a pinned `file:line` ref states).
4. **`price`** (`price:<id>`): check the claim's amount, currency, date and league against the `prices[]`
   entry. An `unavailable` entry is stated as unavailable, with no amount; offline, every entry is
   `unavailable` with note `offline run`. You have no trade tool: never check against the market.
5. **`pob-computed`**: the ref names a field the final record holds, and the claim states its value as the
   text rounds it. A `pob-computed` number resting on a line PoB doesn't apply is a finding: it belongs in
   the ledger.
6. **`ledger`** (`ledger:<id>`): the entry models what the claim says: its `claim`, formula and inputs are
   the quantity the claim states, on the stages it means, and each input's tag supports its value. Don't
   evaluate the formula: the run checker re-evaluates every entry against the record.
7. **`judgment`**: stated with its caveat, never as settled.
8. **`external-unavailable`**: one finding each, `issue` exactly `tagged external-unavailable` and
   `location` the claim's: `accepted-offline` when the run log's mode is `offline`, `unresolved` otherwise
   (C-30). Nothing else is ever `accepted-offline`.
9. **No `source`**: an `unresolved` finding, issue `no source`.

### What PoB applies, and the lookups

- A **partly parsed** line means PoB applies **none** of it; an unparsed line is ignored. A PoB number that
  relies on such a line is wrong, and the ledger must hand-calculate the whole line (D-106).
- **`possibly unread` is advisory** (D-243): the coordinator confirms it by a measurement (two stages
  compiled measure-only). When a hinge figure rests on a `possibly unread` line and no claim records that
  measurement, report a finding for the claim's owner ("possibly unread, not confirmed by measurement"); you
  can't compile, so never guess the number yourself.
- **Lookups** (`mcp__pob2__lookup_game_data`, `mcp__pob2__check_model_coverage`,
  `mcp__pob2__search_tree_nodes`, `mcp__pob2__list_gems`, `mcp__pob2__get_classes`): a unique's full text
  comes only when the lookup returns exactly one unique, so look it up by its exact name (D-239);
  `{mode: "mechanic", query, kind}` lists one kind only (`gem`, `unique`, `passive`, `mod`) when a broad
  query fills every slot with another (D-240); a gem's stat sets show their base attack damage and base
  critical hit chance (D-241).
- **Grounding confidence** (A-29): a value the grounding rates M or L (a waystone modifier's size, the
  interlude +8, the 65+/75+ trial dispute, the Martyr point) is used as recorded; a finding whose check
  rests on one says so in its `evidence`.

### The editor read of `guide.md` (D-279)

Read each H2 against the plan (its claims, stages, ledger and prices) and the record. Compare a guide figure
only with the record's measured values (its stats, `measures` and key figures), rounded as a guide rounds it
("about 2,740"), which a ledger edit can't change; never with a ledger value, which is the run checker's
`[ledger]` (D-365). Flag:

1. **A fact or number not in them**: one the plan or the record doesn't hold, that contradicts them, or that
   states them more strongly (a qualifier its claim doesn't support). A mechanic the guide states must be a
   claim's, and the claim's source must support it.
2. **Advice that asserts a fact.** First-person advice ("I", "my") is advice and isn't verified; but advice
   that asserts a fact the plan lacks ("I find every hit explodes") is a finding.
3. **Tutorial text** (D-280): a sentence that explains how the game or PoB works and doesn't name something
   this build uses, needs or avoids, or change what the reader does (the swap test: it would read as true,
   word for word, in a guide for another skill on another class).
4. **A caveat repeated, or later stated as settled** (D-279): each caveat appears once, where the reader
   acts on it.
5. **The gated statements**:
   - **the named damage source is the main source of damage** (D-122, as D-295 words it): `At a glance` and
     `How to play it` say so, and its figure in the verdict lines is the named source's Count-scaled figure;
     when it is the single-target skill rather than the clear skill, `How to play it` says why (D-148);
   - **estimates** (D-296): each Count above 1 and each custom modifier is listed in `What PoB can't show`
     with the plan's reason; a figure that rests on Count or a hand calculation, or that the record marks
     `computedWithCustomMods`, is stated as an estimate, never as exact; every interaction the guide states
     must still exist in game text (a lookup confirms it);
   - **the budget lines** (D-164, D-219, D-281): the entering-maps total against 5 divines and the budget
     variant's against 20, with "near the ceiling" in those words above 5 (to 5.5) and above 20 (to 22); an
     `unavailable` price means the guide says the prices are unavailable and why, and never claims the rung
     is within its budget; the non-budget total against the ~200-divine reference, never as a cap;
   - **the survival floor** (D-234): with a `chaosResistException`, one line of that rung's `Checks` states
     the record's chaos resistance and the exception's reason;
   - **offline** (D-201): `Your PoB file` says the run was offline and that no price was checked.

**Never flag a missing optional item** (D-277): a fallback, a threshold trigger (a first-person judgment
trigger is fine, and a trigger's form isn't checked), a trap, an either/or pick, a hold or save row, a rotation
clause, a "dormant until" line or a "buy now" line.

### Triage (D-352)

Triage every finding before you write it:

- **Mark it `hinge: yes` or `hinge: no`.** `yes` when what it is about is a hinge fact, whose being wrong
  would make the build fail at its rung: a `hinge` claim, a guide sentence stating one, or a gated statement
  (the guide can't drop those). `no` for everything else: a `reason`, a kill time, campaign resistance
  maths, a minor pick, a timing, wording, rounding or trivia.
- **A `hinge: no` finding is fixed by removing or narrowing the statement, never by new research or a
  recompile.** Its `issue` says which. Don't research a non-hinge statement yourself either: one that the
  plan, the record or a lookup you already made doesn't support is a `hinge: no` finding to remove or
  narrow.
- **A difference under 5% in an estimate is no finding.** An estimate is a figure resting on Count, a hand
  calculation or a ledger entry, one the record marks `computedWithCustomMods`, or one the guide states as
  "about". Within 5% of the plan's or record's value (the difference under 5% of that value), report
  nothing; a larger difference, or an estimate stated as exact, is a finding.

### Findings

One per false or unsupported claim or guide sentence, numbered `v4-<n>-<k>` (this pass `<n>`, `<k>` from
1):

- `status`: `unresolved`, `accepted-offline` (rule 8 above only) or, in a re-check, `fixed`;
- `hinge`: `yes` or `no` (triage);
- `owner`: the step that wrote it: `W1` for `guide.md`; for a plan claim, `D1` when its stage is an endgame
  stage (one with a `variant`: `m`, `b` or `n`) and `D2` when it is a campaign stage. A guide number that
  differs from a right plan or record value is `W1`'s; a wrong claim is its writer's, even when the guide
  renders it faithfully;
- `artifact`: `plan.json` or `guide.md`;
- `location`: `stages[<i>].claims[<k>] (<claim id>)`, or `<H2 title>, line <n>`, the sentence's line in
  the file. A location never quotes the artifact;
- `claim`: the claim's text, or the guide's sentence; `issue`; `evidence` (what you looked up or derived,
  with the numbers);
- `reader`, on every `unresolved` finding and only there (D-265): one plain sentence the guide's reader can
  act on, what is wrong in the guide and what is true instead, with no pipeline words (lens, finding, owner,
  claim, location) and no JSON path: "The Act 1 resistance line is wrong: stage 2 already has 40% fire
  resistance." A finding open after the re-check goes into the guide's `Known issues` as that sentence,
  word for word.

## The full pass and the re-check

- **`check: "full"`**: every hinge claim of `plan.json` and every H2 of `guide.md`, with no sampling.
  `## Checked` lists each hinge claim, `claim stages[<i>].claims[<k>] (<id>)`, and each H2,
  `section <H2 title>`.
- **`check: "recheck"`**: check only
  1. each finding in the header's `findings`: read it in `reports/V4-<n-1>.md` and report it again under the
     same `id`. When `changed` lists its claim or its H2, or its claim's id is gone from `plan.json` (a
     `hinge: no` finding is fixed by removal), check it again: `fixed` when it now holds, or when its claim
     is gone and the guide no longer states it; `unresolved` when it doesn't. When nothing in `changed`
     touches it, nothing changed it: report it `unresolved` again, as it was, without checking it;
  2. each item of `changed`: a claim as the full pass checks a claim of its kind, an H2 as the editor read
     checks it. A new problem is a new finding, `v4-<n>-<k>`.

  `## Checked` lists every item of `changed`, as the header gives it. Nothing else: the rest was checked
  before. This is also a revision's check: a revision runs no full pass. After a fix round's re-check there is
  no further round.

## Writes

`reports/V4-<n>.md`, the one file your header's `writes` names, with one Write, and nothing else (D-352).
**Never overwrite a report:** first Glob `reports/V4-*.md`; if your file exists, write nothing and return
`exists: reports/V4-<n>.md`.

The report is markdown in this exact form, so the runner and the run checker read it by line. Each field is
one `- <key>: <value>` line with its value on that line; `## Findings` holds one H3 per finding, its id, or
the one line `None.`.

```markdown
# V4-2: re-check of rounds/1

- check: recheck
- concept: r1-a-2
- mode: offline
- verdict: fail

## Checked

- claim stages[3].claims[1] (m-trap-weapon)
- section How to play it

## Findings

### v4-1-3

- status: unresolved
- hinge: yes
- owner: D1
- artifact: plan.json
- location: stages[3].claims[1] (m-trap-weapon)
- claim: A main-hand weapon switches the skill off.
- issue: the skill's text limits this to a two-handed weapon; the claim says any main-hand weapon
- evidence: mcp__pob2__lookup_game_data gem "…" lists "…"; the claim's source states the same
- reader: The weapon warning is too broad: only a two-handed weapon switches the skill off.
```

- The H1 is `# V4-<n>: full pass` or `# V4-<n>: re-check of rounds/<n-1>`.
- `check` is the header's; `mode` is the run log's; `verdict` is `pass` exactly when no finding is
  `unresolved`.
- A finding's fields are in this order: `status`, `hinge`, `owner`, `artifact`, `location`, `claim`,
  `issue`, `evidence`, and `reader` on an `unresolved` finding only. A `fixed` or `accepted-offline` finding
  has no `reader`. Only a re-check reports `fixed`.

## Returns

At most 8 lines:

```
wrote: reports/V4-<n>.md
verdict: fail
unresolved: 2 (1 hinge), accepted-offline: 1, fixed: 3
```

or `exists: reports/V4-<n>.md` when that report was already there.

## Fault directives

None. The seeded checks of AC-W15 are applied by the build coordinator to a copy of a run folder, not
through the skill: a copy whose run log says `online` (FI-5) must make every `external-unavailable` claim's
finding `unresolved`. A ledger formula wrapped as `(<formula>) * 2` (FI-4) is the run checker's to catch,
since it re-evaluates the ledger; V4 reports it only when a hinge claim cites that entry and the formula no
longer models the claim.
