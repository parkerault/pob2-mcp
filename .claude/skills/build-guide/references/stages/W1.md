# W1: write the guide and the audit layer (`build-guide-writer`)

Slice 03 §5.3 step 5, §7.8 and §8; spine D-101, D-122, D-164, D-205, D-219, D-234, D-276…D-283, D-293, D-294,
D-295, D-301. W1 writes two files. `guide.md` is the reader layer: a game guide a player who has never seen the
build follows from level 1 through the campaign to the endgame (D-101), about 2,840 words. `audit.md` is the audit layer: what the agents
keep and no player is expected to read (D-278). Everything in both comes from the run's artifacts: the plan,
the final compile record (its ledger values included), the dossier, the run log and the D1 gate's verdict file
the prompt names. The guide renders the plan: you add no fact or number of your own (D-276). The V4 verifiers
check the plan, and read the guide like an editor, after you.

## Inputs

- **Header:** `stage: "W1"`, `attempt`, `concept`, `mode`, `runFolder`, `runFolderRel`, `compile: "none"`,
  `writes: ["guide.md", "audit.md"]`, and:
  - `findings`: on a re-run, a note that an earlier attempt returned without its files (they may hold its
    partial work: write them whole); in the fix round, the V4 findings routed to this step; after a revision,
    the user's requests (`code: "user-request"`);
  - `changed`, in the fix round or a revision: the locations D1 and D2 changed, or `"all"` when a gate
    re-ran (an owner recompiled, so the record and the import code are new);
  - `accepted`: gate findings the user accepted at an escalation. Each goes under `Known issues`, in both files.
- **Prompt:** the D1 gate's last verdict file, `reports/gates/D1-gate-<concept>-a<n>.json` (its budget
  totals and rates in `basis.budget`, the named damage source's figures in `basis.rolledSkill`, and its floor,
  budget and `rolledSkill` findings), and whether the league couldn't be verified.
- **The fix round and revisions** (D-264). You never write the guide anew: fix it in place with Edit. Fix
  every place each of your findings touches, not only the flagged location. Then patch every place the guide
  renders a claim at a `changed` location, so it says what the plan now says. With `changed: "all"`, also copy
  the new record's `importCode` into `Your PoB file` and bring every number the guide and `audit.md` quote, the
  verdict lines' included, up to the current record and plan. The re-check reads what you changed. Leave the
  lines under the guide's `### Known issues` and the rows of `audit.md`'s `Known issues` to the runner's step
  14, which replaces them whole, except to add a gate finding newly in `accepted`.
- **Read:**
  - the templates `.claude/skills/build-guide/templates/guide-template.md` and
    `.claude/skills/build-guide/templates/audit-template.md`;
  - `plan.json` (stages, `claims`, `ledger`, `prices`, `versions`, `league`, every `chaosResistException`
    and `respec`, and each group's `count` and `countReason` and each `config.customMods` entry with its
    reason);
  - `compile-record.json`, the final compile's record (C-33): each stage's `loadoutName`, `level`,
    `outputs` (`TotalEHP` among them), `skillOutputs` and the Count-scaled `countedSkillOutputs`, each stage's
    `computedWithCustomMods` mark, the `ledger` values, `engine`, `pobFile`, `importCode`, `versionNotes`,
    `loadoutWarnings` and `saveWarnings` (a large record: Read it in parts, or Grep the fields you need);
  - `dossier.md` (the card's entry, the mechanics), the card `cards/<id>.md`, and `run-log.json` (the mode,
    the date, and `checkpoints[]`: the verification gate's entry that finalized this card, which is the only
    record of why it was picked; the dossier is written before that gate and doesn't say);
  - the C1/E1 facts under `data/` that a claim cites.

## Work: the guide

Write `guide.md` from its template, section by section (§8 says what each holds, and the template gives each
section's word budget). The run checker holds the guide to rules 1 to 4; the V4 `prose` lens reads it against
the rest.

1. **Headings.** One H1 naming the roll (`<Ascendancy> / <Skill>: <concept title>`), then exactly the
   template's fourteen H2s, in its order, and no other H2. Under `Leveling`, one H3 per campaign stage (a
   stage without `variant`), in plan order, titled with its loadout name **exactly** as the record's
   `stages.<id>.loadoutName` has it, and no other H3. Each `Endgame` H2 has its H3 `Checks`; `What PoB can't
   show` ends with the H3 `Known issues`.
2. **The cap.** At most 3,000 words, the H1, the import code and the Known issues list not counted (D-283).
   Aim at the template's budgets, about 2,840 in all.
3. **No pipeline text** (D-276). No footnote markers (`[^…]`), no confidence labels ("confidence L"), no
   finding ids, no pipeline words (lens, finding, owner, claim, location) and no stage ids outside the
   loadout names. The plan's claims carry the guide's sources; `audit.md` keeps the confidence.
4. **The verdict lines** (D-283). `Survival by stage` has one line per endgame rung in the template's fixed
   form, word for word but the placeholders, and the run checker compares each number with the record:
   - the level is the stage's `level`; the resistances are the record's `FireResist`, `ColdResist`,
     `LightningResist` and `ChaosResist` at −60%;
   - "Max hit <n> physical against about <hit>": the record's `PhysicalMaximumHitTaken`, against the ledger
     value of `hit_<stage>_physical`;
   - "at least <n> elemental or chaos against about <hit>": the least of the four other
     `<Type>MaximumHitTaken`, against the largest of the four other `hit_<stage>_<type>` ledger values;
   - "<Skill> about <n> DPS": the named damage source's Count-scaled figure the D1 gate's verdict names for
     the stage (`basis.rolledSkill`, the `rolledGroup`'s figure), read from the record's
     `countedSkillOutputs[g<group>s<skill>p<statSet>].fields.<Field>`: the headline, the same number PoB's Full
     DPS shows for the named source (D-294);
   - "Effective HP about <n>.", which ends the line: the stage's `TotalEHP` (D-294). It stands beside max hit,
     never alone, and max hit stays the basis of the verdict word.

   Round each number as a guide does, at its last significant digit: 2,736 is "2,740" or "2,700", 401.996 is
   "about 400", 438.55 is "about 440". Quote a reference hit rounded from its ledger value (`audit.md` keeps
   the value itself), never as the scenario's whole number (439), which is that value rounded up for PoB
   (D-256). The verdict word (`solid`, `watch`, `risky`) is your judgment of the line's numbers.
5. **Facts and advice** (D-276). A fact is a plain statement of something the plan or the record holds, and
   numbers come from them, rounded as above. Advice is in the first person ("I drop Herald of Ash first", "my
   support order"): that is its label, and it isn't verified, but a number inside it is still the plan's or
   the record's, and it never asserts a fact the plan lacks.
6. **Each caveat once** (D-279). A caveat (a source of confidence M or L, a disputed fact, a judgment, a
   mechanic no lookup confirmed, what PoB doesn't model) appears once, where the reader acts on it, usually in
   `What PoB can't show` by the figure it qualifies. A later mention doesn't hedge it again, and never states
   it as settled. State a mechanic as reliable only when the build has a source for it.
7. **Not a tutorial** (D-280). A sentence that explains how the game or PoB works stays only when it names
   something this build uses, needs or avoids (its skill, supports, nodes, key items, an attribute or level
   requirement, or a plan number) and it changes what the reader does with this build. The swap test: if the
   sentence would read as true and useful, word for word, in a guide for another skill on another class, cut
   it. Keep the consequence, not the rule ("Blood Hunt needs a level-7 uncut gem", not how uncut gems work).
   Campaign practicals are actions, not explanations.
8. **Your PoB file.** The import code goes in this section's first fenced block, copied character for
   character from the record's `importCode` (D-205). Then the file name; the loadouts as one ordered line,
   each with its trigger; that the items are stat examples; the engine version (the record's `engine` and the
   plan's `enginePatch`) and the boss data's patch (`externalPatch`); in an offline run, that it was made
   offline and no price was checked (the word "offline" must appear here). **The league** is `plan.json`'s
   `league`: `unverified` there means trade was unreachable when the endgame was priced, and the guide says
   the league couldn't be verified. Then the record's PoB-file notes, each once, in plain words:
   `versionNotes`; `loadoutWarnings` (PoB2's loadout switch can drop an off-hand that Giant's Blood,
   Instruments of Power or Lord of the Wilds makes legal: tell the reader to check Weapon 2 and Weapon 2 Swap
   after switching and re-equip it); `saveWarnings` (saving in PoB2 drops some config options: set them
   again).
9. **Leveling** (at most 110 words a stage): the setup, the tree additions with their reasons, attributes
   (have and need), gear targets, when to swap, and any respec with its gold cost (D-144).
   **Campaign route and rewards** (D-265, D-283): the reward quests in route order. For each reward choice the
   plan picks (a stage's `config.scenario` entry citing `campaign/progress.json#q.<key>.options[<i>]`), name
   the quest, then the picked option by its first line exactly as the fact lists it (the run checker looks
   for it there), then the reason the plan's claim gives. Never list a choice's options without its pick.
10. **The endgame rungs** (D-281). `Endgame: entering maps` is stage `m`, `Endgame: budget` stage `b`,
    `Endgame: non-budget` stage `n`. Each H2 opens with its one "Move here when" line. Entering maps gives the
    changes from the Epilogue and D1's numbered buy order within 5 divines; budget its buy order, a slot table
    (slot, affix target as text, fallback) and the gems and tree with their reasons; non-budget its upgrade
    order. Where the plan's claim names an affix's source that limits the base, the target says so. Where a
    stage has a `respec` (from the rung before it on the ladder, D-282), a respec line gives the dropped nodes
    and the gold cost. Each `Checks` holds what the verdict line doesn't:
    - **the chaos exception**, when the stage has a `chaosResistException`: **one line** holds both the
      record's `ChaosResist` for that stage, as a number with `%`, and the exception's `reason` copied in full,
      word for word: "Chaos resistance stays at 28% because no slot can carry chaos resistance without
      dropping a core item or breaking the budget." Don't wrap that line (D-234);
    - **the budget line** (D-164, D-219, D-281), from the verdict file's `basis.budget`: entering maps' total
      against 5 divines, the budget variant's against 20. When the entering-maps total is over 5 and at most
      5.5, or the budget total over 20 and at most 22, that `Checks` says **near the ceiling**, in those words.
      When any price is `unavailable`, say the prices are unavailable and why, and never claim the rung is
      within its budget. The non-budget total is set against the ~200-divine reference, never as a cap (D-117);
    - for `b` and `n` only, **the Arbiter of Divinity** (D-116, A-30): its hits against max hit taken and the
      time-to-kill range, and **the mapping signals** (D-121, D-132). `m` gets neither.
11. **How to play it**: clear and single target; the named damage source as the main damage (D-122), and why
    when it isn't the clear skill (D-148).
12. **What PoB can't show**: each caveat once, by the figure it qualifies, and the settings the headline
    figures assume, from the plan's config (charges, stacks, counts). Each Count above 1 and each custom
    modifier, with the reason the plan gives it (`countReason`, `customMods[].reason`), rendered from the plan
    (D-294): the figures they touch are estimates (D-296). Its last part is always the H3 `Known issues` (see
    "Known issues" below).
13. **Optional items** (D-277), only where the build has them, never invented to fill a slot: a fallback D1 or
    D2 already weighed; a threshold trigger, when the plan has one (else a first-person judgment trigger,
    "when you can afford it"); up to 4 traps in How to play it, each confirmed by item text, gem text or PoB;
    up to 2 either/or picks in the FAQ, each with its deciding factor as a PoB damage or survival difference
    (and the price difference online); hold and save rows in the timing table; a rotation clause for a
    leveling stage whose damage is a combo; a "dormant until" line in Leveling; a "buy now, works at <loadout>"
    line in Priorities. Each needs a plan claim, or is first-person advice.
14. **Held back:** crafting recipes, an atlas section, affix tier labels, trade links and a vendor search
    regex.
15. **Changelog**: one dated entry for this version.

## Work: the audit layer

Write `audit.md` from its template (§7.8, D-278), with its six H2s in order: `Hand calculations` (each ledger
entry's id, claim, value from the record and formula in words, D-106), `Stage detail` (the per-loadout numbers
from the record, the resistances by stage, each reference hit's ledger value, the mana and leech pairs, the
Arbiter and mapping detail), `What PoB doesn't model` (`mcp__pob2__check_model_coverage`'s flags from the
dossier and plan, and how the guide handles each), `Inputs by confidence` (the M and L inputs, A-29; offline,
the claims tagged `external-unavailable`), `Run notes` and `Known issues`.

**Why this concept**, in `Run notes`, says only what `run-log.json`'s verification gate entry whose `answer`
finalized the card (`pick:<id>`; the last such entry) records: with `answeredBy` `user`, that the user picked it
at the verification gate; with `pre-supplied` or `default`, that the test config picked it, quoting the entry's
`rule` exactly (`first-viable`). Give no reason the entry doesn't record, and never invent one. `Run notes` also give
the D1 gate's budget totals and rates.

## Known issues (D-265, D-276)

The guide's `### Known issues` lists plain sentences, one per line, with no id: each says what is wrong in the
guide and what is true instead, with no pipeline words and no JSON paths. `audit.md`'s `Known issues` table
holds each one's row: `| <id> | <lens or gate> | <stage> | <the sentence the guide prints> |`.

- You write each accepted gate finding: its sentence in the guide, and its row in `audit.md`, with its code
  as the id, its gate (`D1-gate` or `D2-gate`) and its plan stage:
  `- The budget variant spends more passive points than level 90 allows, so PoB2 shows it over the limit.` and
  `| level-points | D1-gate | b | The budget variant spends more passive points than level 90 allows, so PoB2 shows it over the limit. |`.
  With none, each list holds the one line `None.`
- The runner adds each V4 finding left open after the re-check: the verifier's `reader` sentence word for word
  in the guide, and its row `| <id> | <lens> | — | <reader> |` in `audit.md` (SKILL.md step 14).

The runner finds both lists by the line numbers you return, so return them after your first write and after
every patch, as the lines of the headings in the files as you leave them.

## Writes

`guide.md` and `audit.md` only, and no other file: no notes, drafts or scratch files, in the run folder or
anywhere else. Write each with Write; to correct it afterwards, fix it in place with Edit rather than writing
it again (D-257). You can't change the plan, the record or the dossier: if one looks wrong, write what it says
and report the problem in `gaps`.

## Returns

At most 8 lines: `guide.md` and `audit.md`; `known-issues-line: <n>`, the line number of the guide's
`### Known issues` heading, and `audit-known-issues-line: <n>`, that of `audit.md`'s `## Known issues`, both
as you leave the files, on your first write and on every patch (the runner Reads from them at step 14); each
gap, one line, what you couldn't fill and why (a record field that is missing, a plan claim with no source),
or `gaps: none`; and in the fix round or a revision, the finding ids you fixed and the locations you changed
(`guide.md How to play it`). No import code: you copied it into the guide, and `compile-record.json` keeps it
(D-260).

```
guide.md, audit.md
known-issues-line: 118
audit-known-issues-line: 64
gaps: none
```
